# ADR: Permissionless Uniswap v4 protocol pools

- Status: Accepted and implemented
- Date: 2026-08-21
- Scope: Statics pool creation, PoolKey policy, Statics fee configuration, creator revenue, permanent liquidity, LP rewards, governance, indexing, routing, and DEX market structure
- Supersedes: `docs/adr/governed-protocol-pools.md` where the decisions conflict

## Context

Statics currently recognizes basket canonical pools and governance-created protocol pools.

Basket canonical pools are created as part of basket launch. Each pool uses the installed `StaticsSwapFeeHook`, has zero native Uniswap v4 LP fee, participates in bilateral Statics swap-fee routing, and launches with creator-funded full-range permanent liquidity.

The governed protocol-pool architecture later generalized the surrounding liquidity system so governance could create a pool between arbitrary compatible ERC-20 assets without pretending the pool belonged to a basket. Governance pools use the same PoolManager, PositionManager, Statics hook, permanent-liquidity accounting, LP-reward accounting, PositionNFT custody, and global Statics-staker reward system as basket canonical pools.

That architecture established most of the generic machinery required for a broader Statics market layer, but intentionally retained several restrictions:

- only governance may create a non-basket pool;
- tick spacing is fixed by the protocol;
- creation requires an initial permanent-liquidity seed;
- general pools are identified as governance pools rather than ordinary Statics markets; and
- pool creators have no persistent economic participation in the markets they establish.

These restrictions are no longer desirable.

Statics is intended to operate as a Uniswap v4-native DEX layer in addition to its basket and Statics Dollar primitives. Basket markets remain first-class protocol markets, but there is no architectural reason to restrict the shared Statics hook, swap-fee system, protocol-owned-liquidity flywheel, LP rewards, or global Statics-staker rewards to basket-associated or governance-selected pairs.

Statics also does not need to adopt native Uniswap v4 LP fees to provide configurable market fee levels. The existing architecture already supports a stronger separation:

- native Uniswap v4 LP fee is zero;
- Statics charges its own bilateral hook fee;
- fee allocation is controlled by Statics;
- eligible LPs receive their compensation from the Statics fee allocation;
- permanent liquidity grows from the same Statics fee stream; and
- PoolId-specific Statics fee configuration already exists.

Maintaining zero native LP fees avoids operating two overlapping fee systems and keeps the market's complete economic policy inside Statics.

Permissionless pools therefore vary by token pair and valid tick spacing, while their swap-fee rate is configured at the Statics hook layer.

A creator establishing a new general pool pays the configured pool creation fee and permanently receives 5% of the Statics bilateral swap fees generated by that pool.

This ADR replaces the existing governed generic-pool creation model rather than adding a third pool class beside it.

## Decision

Statics will recognize two primary classes of registered protocol pool:

1. **Basket canonical pool**: the existing BasketToken/constituent pool associated with one basket and one constituent.
2. **General pool**: a registered Statics-hook pool between two compatible ERC-20 assets with no basket association.

A general pool may be created permissionlessly when permissionless creation is enabled.

The governing PoolKey rule is:

> Any unique supported Uniswap v4 PoolKey consisting of two compatible ERC-20 currencies, zero native v4 LP fee, a valid creator-selected tick spacing, and the installed Statics hook may be registered and initialized as a Statics general pool.

Every Statics protocol pool therefore uses:

```solidity
PoolKey({
    currency0: sortedCurrency0,
    currency1: sortedCurrency1,
    fee: 0,
    tickSpacing: selectedTickSpacing,
    hooks: IHooks(installedStaticsHook)
})
```

Native Uniswap v4 LP fees remain disabled.

The Statics bilateral hook fee is the sole swap-fee layer used for protocol economics.

General pool creators select an initial Statics fee rate during creation, subject to protocol-defined fee bounds. The creator does not select or control the allocation of that fee between LP rewards, permanent liquidity, staking, creator revenue, and treasury.

Different PoolKeys for the same economic pair are allowed when their tick spacing differs.

For example:

```text
NVDA / USDC / fee=0 / tickSpacing=10  / Statics hook -> PoolId A
NVDA / USDC / fee=0 / tickSpacing=60  / Statics hook -> PoolId B
NVDA / USDC / fee=0 / tickSpacing=200 / Statics hook -> PoolId C
```

Each pool may have a different Statics bilateral fee rate:

```text
PoolId A -> Statics fee rate A
PoolId B -> Statics fee rate B
PoolId C -> Statics fee rate C
```

The Statics fee rate is not part of the Uniswap v4 PoolKey. Two pools with the same currencies, same tick spacing, zero native fee, and the same Statics hook are therefore the same PoolId even if callers would prefer different Statics fee rates.

Statics does not introduce cloned hooks, custom salts, synthetic PoolIds, or other mechanisms solely to create exact PoolKey duplicates.

General market creation is separate from liquidity provision.

A creator is not required to permanently seed the pool during creation. External liquidity may be provided after initialization.

Ordinary Uniswap v4 liquidity may technically be added outside the Statics Diamond, but because every Statics PoolKey has zero native LP fee, such liquidity receives no native v4 swap-fee income.

To receive the Statics LP fee allocation, an LP position must satisfy Statics eligibility requirements and be staked through the Statics Diamond under a PositionNFT.

Statics therefore economically, rather than artificially, enforces its LP system.

## Objectives

- Allow general Statics markets to be created without governance approval while permissionless creation is enabled.
- Preserve a controlled launch phase using the creation fee as the permissionless-creation gate.
- Keep native Uniswap v4 LP fees permanently zero for Statics protocol pools.
- Make Statics bilateral hook fees the sole protocol swap-fee system.
- Allow creators to select a valid tick spacing for general pools.
- Allow creators to select an initial Statics fee rate within protocol-defined bounds.
- Preserve protocol control over fee allocation shares.
- Allow multiple independent markets for the same pair when their tick spacing differs.
- Reject exact PoolKey duplicates.
- Permanently route 5% of each pool's Statics bilateral fees to its creator.
- Preserve pull-based creator revenue accounting.
- Require Statics-reward-eligible LP positions to use full-range liquidity.
- Route rewarded LP activity through the existing Diamond and PositionNFT system.
- Preserve pool-local LP-reward accounting.
- Preserve pool-local permanent-liquidity accounting and fee-funded compounding.
- Allow the same reward asset generated by multiple pools to converge into the global Statics-staker reward system.
- Preserve basket-specific backing, basket rewards, lending, and ExitOnly behavior only for basket canonical pools.
- Separate market creation from mandatory liquidity funding.
- Emit sufficient events for indexers to discover, classify, and route across Statics markets.
- Prefer the intended final architecture over preserving obsolete testnet ABI compatibility.
- Keep all Statics-owned implementation code compatible with the repository's default non-IR compiler profile and EIP-170 runtime limits.

## Non-goals

- Supporting nonzero native Uniswap v4 LP fees.
- Supporting dynamic native v4 LP fees.
- Allowing arbitrary hooks.
- Creating multiple pools with an identical PoolKey.
- Introducing cloned Statics hooks to manufacture additional PoolIds.
- Native ETH pools. WETH is used where an ETH-denominated ERC-20 is required.
- Supporting fee-on-transfer, rebasing, callback-mutating, or otherwise balance-incompatible tokens as a guaranteed protocol feature.
- Treating pool creation as token endorsement.
- Treating a pool's spot price, TWAP, or liquidity as an oracle for Statics Dollar, baskets, lending, or collateral admission.
- Automatically admitting a pool currency as Dollar collateral, basket constituent, borrowable asset, or another risk-bearing protocol role.
- Giving general pools basket backing or basket-staker rewards.
- Allowing pool creators to change fee allocation shares.
- Allowing pool creators to release permanent liquidity.
- Allowing pool creators to pause or decommission markets.
- Blocking users from donating economically uncompensated liquidity directly through compatible Uniswap v4 tooling.
- Rewarding concentrated LP positions through Statics LP rewards.
- Rebuilding Uniswap's tick-aware concentrated-liquidity fee accounting inside Statics.
- Changing the separate Doppler Genesis launch architecture.

## Terminology

- **Protocol pool**: a PoolKey recognized by the Diamond and registered with the installed Statics hook.
- **Basket canonical pool**: a protocol pool associated with one basket and one constituent.
- **General pool**: a protocol pool with no basket association.
- **Permissionless creation**: the ability for a non-governance caller to create a general pool by paying the configured creation fee.
- **Pool creator**: the immutable address entitled to the creator allocation from a protocol pool's Statics bilateral swap fees.
- **Pool creation fee**: the governance-configured native-currency fee that both enables permissionless general-pool creation and discourages frivolous market creation.
- **Native v4 LP fee**: the Uniswap v4 LP fee encoded in `PoolKey.fee`. It is always zero for Statics protocol pools.
- **Statics fee rate**: the bilateral swap fee charged through `StaticsSwapFeeHook`, including its supported input-side and output-side fee configuration.
- **Fee allocation profile**: the protocol-controlled allocation of collected Statics fees between permanent liquidity, LP rewards, basket rewards where applicable, Statics staking, creator revenue, and treasury.
- **Permanent liquidity**: hook-owned full-range liquidity funded from the Statics fee stream.
- **External LP position**: a Uniswap v4 PositionManager NFT that has not been staked through Statics.
- **Statics LP position**: an eligible PositionManager NFT staked under a Statics PositionNFT and participating in Statics LP rewards.
- **Active pool**: a registered and initialized protocol pool that has not been decommissioned.

## Protocol pool resolution

The normalized protocol-pool registry resolves both basket canonical and general pools.

The intended normalized types are:

```solidity
enum ProtocolPoolKind {
    None,
    BasketCanonical,
    General
}

struct GeneralPool {
    PoolKey key;
    address creator;
    bool registered;
}

struct ProtocolPoolView {
    PoolId poolId;
    PoolKey key;
    ProtocolPoolKind kind;
    bool decommissioned;
    uint256 basketId;
    address basketAsset;
    address creator;
    uint128 permanentLiquidity;
}
```

Resolution follows these rules:

1. If an existing basket pool association resolves to the supplied PoolId, the pool is `BasketCanonical`.
2. Otherwise, if the general-pool registry contains that PoolId and its stored PoolKey hashes to it, the pool is `General`.
3. Otherwise, it is not a Statics protocol pool.

A PoolId may belong to exactly one class.

Both basket creation and general pool creation must reject a PoolId already claimed by either class.

The creator resolver is normalized:

```text
BasketCanonical -> basket creator
General         -> stored general-pool creator
```

Downstream fee-routing code consumes the normalized creator rather than duplicating pool-class-specific lookup logic.

## Replacement of the governed generic-pool model

This ADR replaces the existing governed generic-pool creation path.

The implementation should not add `General` as a third class beside `Governance`.

The intended type and symbol replacement is:

```text
ProtocolPoolKind.Governance
    -> ProtocolPoolKind.General

GovernancePool storage
    -> GeneralPool storage with immutable creator

quoteGovernancePool
    -> quotePool

createGovernancePool
    -> createPool

decommissionGovernancePool
    -> decommissionGeneralPool
```

The existing generic-pool creation parameters used only for mandatory permanent-liquidity seeding are removed from base pool creation:

```text
payer
amountAMax
amountBMax
minLiquidity
```

General market creation no longer requires permanent-liquidity funding.

Existing governed-pool tests and implementation should be refactored where useful rather than preserved as a separate feature rail.

## PoolKey policy

Statics constructs the PoolKey rather than accepting arbitrary hook or fee fields from the caller.

The creator supplies:

- two token addresses;
- tick spacing;
- initial price;
- initial Statics fee rate;
- creator identity; and
- creation deadline.

Statics determines:

```text
currency ordering
native v4 fee = 0
hook = installed Statics hook
```

A supported general pool must satisfy:

```text
tokenA != tokenB
tokenA != address(0)
tokenB != address(0)
both currencies are supported ERC-20 contract addresses
tickSpacing is valid for PoolManager
PoolId is not already registered
PoolId is not already initialized
```

Native currency is not supported.

The PoolManager remains responsible for its native tick-spacing and initialization validity checks. The Diamond must perform its own registration and policy checks before calling PoolManager.

## Tick spacing and market identity

Tick spacing is creator-selectable because it is part of PoolKey and therefore provides a native Uniswap v4 mechanism for establishing distinct markets.

For example:

```text
TOKEN / WETH / tickSpacing=10
TOKEN / WETH / tickSpacing=60
TOKEN / WETH / tickSpacing=200
```

are independent PoolIds.

Each has independent:

```text
price state
Uniswap liquidity
Statics LP reward accounting
Statics fee rate
permanent-liquidity inventory
permanent-liquidity position
creator identity
pool lifecycle state
```

A Statics fee-rate difference alone does not produce a distinct PoolId.

Therefore:

```text
same currencies
+ same tickSpacing
+ fee=0
+ same Statics hook
= same PoolId
```

An attempted second creation reverts regardless of the requested Statics fee rate or initial price.

## Statics fee rate

General pool creators select the initial Statics fee rate when creating a pool.

The supported rate consists of the hook's input-side and output-side fee parameters:

```solidity
struct PoolSwapFeeRate {
    uint16 inputFeeBps;
    uint16 outputFeeBps;
}
```

The requested rate must satisfy the same canonical constraint enforced by `StaticsSwapFeeHook`:

```text
inputFeeBps + outputFeeBps <= 200
```

The Diamond and hook must use one shared pure fee-policy implementation. The Diamond must not maintain an independent copy of the bound that can diverge from hook validation.

The creator controls only the initial fee **rate**.

The creator does not control:

```text
POL share
LP-reward share
basket-staker share
Statics-staker share
creator share
treasury share
```

Those allocations are configurable protocol policy.

The rate selected during creation is included in creator authorization so a third party cannot front-run a pending creation and alter the intended market fee.

During the governed phase, governance may update a PoolId's Statics fee rate within the same canonical bound.

The pool creator receives no post-creation fee-administration authority merely by being creator.

A later progressive-immutability decision may constrain or remove governance's ability to modify existing pool fee rates.

## Fee configuration separation

Pool fee rate and fee allocation are separate policy dimensions.

The creator share is permanently fixed at:

```solidity
uint16 internal constant CREATOR_SHARE_BPS = 500;
```

It is not included in a governance-mutable allocation structure. Governance configures the remaining 9,500 bps through separate global basket and general allocation profiles:

```solidity
struct BasketFeeAllocation {
    uint16 polShareBps;
    uint16 liquidityProviderShareBps;
    uint16 basketStakerShareBps;
    uint16 staticsStakerShareBps;
    uint16 treasuryShareBps;
}

struct GeneralFeeAllocation {
    uint16 polShareBps;
    uint16 liquidityProviderShareBps;
    uint16 staticsStakerShareBps;
    uint16 treasuryShareBps;
}
```

Every stored profile must satisfy:

```text
configurable shares = 9,500 bps
configurable shares + fixed creator share = 10,000 bps
```

The initial profiles are:

| Allocation | Basket canonical pool | General pool |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Permanent liquidity | 1,000 bps | 3,500 bps |
| Eligible Statics LPs | 2,500 bps | 2,500 bps |
| Basket stakers | 2,500 bps | 0 bps |
| Global Statics stakers | 1,500 bps | 1,500 bps |
| Creator, fixed | 500 bps | 500 bps |
| Treasury | 2,000 bps | 2,000 bps |
| **Total** | **10,000 bps** | **10,000 bps** |

The creator allocation comes from the prior treasury allocation. The initial general-pool POL share is 3,500 bps because the existing governance-pool path effectively routes the unavailable 2,500-bps basket share to POL in addition to the ordinary 1,000-bps POL share.

Governance may update the basket and general allocation profiles independently, subject to the 9,500-bps configurable-share invariant. A profile update affects subsequent fee accrual only. It does not rewrite accrued creator credits, LP rewards, basket rewards, Statics-staker rewards, treasury revenue, or POL inventory.

Changing a global allocation profile does not change any PoolId's fee rate. Changing a PoolId's fee rate does not change the applicable allocation profile.

The fallback policy remains explicit:

- an unavailable LP allocation routes to PoolId-local POL;
- an unavailable basket-staker allocation routes to PoolId-local POL;
- an unavailable global Statics-staker allocation routes to treasury; and
- the creator allocation always credits the immutable creator and never falls back.

## Creation fee and permissionless creation gate

General pools have their own creation fee. It is independent from the basket creation fee and PositionNFT creation fee.

The configured `poolCreationFeeAmount` acts as both:

1. the economic anti-spam cost of public market creation; and
2. the permissionless-creation enable switch.

The rules are:

| Creation fee | Caller | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0` | Diamond owner / governance | Allowed with `msg.value == 0` |
| `0` | Other caller | Revert |
| `> 0` | Any caller | Exact creation fee required |

Zero therefore means:

> **Permissionless pool creation disabled.**

It never means free public creation.

When the fee is nonzero, every caller, including the Diamond owner, must pay the exact configured amount. Underpayment and overpayment revert.

The creation fee is transferred atomically to the configured treasury. A failed transfer reverts the complete creation.

The governed surface includes distinct pool-fee administration and view selectors, conceptually:

```solidity
function setPoolCreationFee(uint256 amount) external;
function poolCreationFee() external view returns (uint256 amount);
```

Canonical deployment initializes this value independently, using a separate deployment setting such as `POOL_CREATION_FEE_AMOUNT`.

Changing the fee affects only future creation. It does not modify existing markets, swaps, creator rights, LP positions, rewards, or permanent liquidity.

A separate permissionless-creation boolean is intentionally not introduced.

## General pool quote and creation interface

The permissionless interface preserves the repository's struct-based, legacy-codegen-resilient style.

A deterministic quote surface allows frontends, launchpads, relayers, and creators to derive the sorted PoolKey, PoolId, normalized initial price, required creation fee, and exact authorization digest before obtaining creator authorization.

The intended surface is conceptually:

```solidity
struct PoolSwapFeeRate {
    uint16 inputFeeBps;
    uint16 outputFeeBps;
}

struct CreatePoolParams {
    address tokenA;
    address tokenB;
    int24 tickSpacing;
    uint160 sqrtPriceBPerAX96;
    PoolSwapFeeRate feeRate;
    address creator;
    uint256 nonce;
    uint256 deadline;
}

struct GeneralPoolQuote {
    PoolKey key;
    PoolId poolId;
    uint160 sqrtPriceX96;
    PoolSwapFeeRate feeRate;
    uint256 creationFee;
    bytes32 authorizationDigest;
}

function quotePool(CreatePoolParams calldata params)
    external
    view
    returns (GeneralPoolQuote memory quote);

function createPool(
    CreatePoolParams calldata params,
    bytes calldata creatorAuthorization
)
    external
    payable
    returns (PoolId poolId);
```

The Diamond sorts the currencies and converts the supplied tokenA/tokenB price into PoolKey orientation using the same raw-unit conventions as the existing governed-pool path. The authorization digest commits to normalized PoolKey orientation rather than caller-selected token ordering.

`quotePool` and `createPool` must use the same validation and normalization path. They enforce at least:

```text
tokenA and tokenB are distinct nonzero contract addresses
native currency is unsupported
1 <= tickSpacing <= 32,767
TickMath.MIN_SQRT_PRICE <= normalized sqrtPriceX96 < TickMath.MAX_SQRT_PRICE
PoolKey.fee == 0
PoolKey.hooks == installed Statics hook
inputFeeBps + outputFeeBps <= 200
PoolId is absent from basket and general registries
PoolId is absent from hook registration
PoolManager has not initialized the PoolId
deadline has not expired
```

The PoolManager remains the final authority on Uniswap initialization validity, but deterministic quote validation must reject the same known policy-invalid tick spacing and price bounds before authorization is requested.

The precise ABI may change during implementation. The invariants defined by this ADR are authoritative.

## Creator attribution and front-running protection

Creator identity carries perpetual economic value and cannot safely be assigned solely to whichever transaction initializes the PoolId first.

Statics uses EIP-712 creator authorization with the Diamond as verifying contract. OpenZeppelin `SignatureChecker` provides EOA and ERC-1271 validation.

The signed authorization binds:

```text
PoolId
normalized sqrtPriceX96
inputFeeBps
outputFeeBps
creator
nonce
deadline
```

The EIP-712 domain binds:

```text
name = "Statics Protocol Pools"
version = "1"
chainId
verifyingContract = Statics Diamond
```

Authorization follows three paths:

1. If `creator == msg.sender`, an empty authorization is accepted for direct creation and no nonce is consumed.
2. If `poolCreationFeeAmount == 0` and `msg.sender` is the Diamond owner, governance may designate a creator without a signature.
3. Otherwise, the named creator must provide a valid EIP-712 authorization and its nonce is consumed.

The owner receives no signature bypass while permissionless creation is enabled unless the owner is also the creator.

Relayed authorization deliberately does not bind `msg.sender`. A copied transaction may cause another caller to pay the creation fee and initialize the exact authorized pool first, but it cannot replace the creator or change the PoolId, normalized price, or fee rate.

Relayed authorizations use unordered creator nonces:

```solidity
mapping(address creator => mapping(uint256 nonce => bool used))
    poolCreationNonceUsed;
```

A creator may cancel an unused authorization without imposing execution order on other authorizations:

```solidity
function invalidatePoolCreationNonce(uint256 nonce) external;
function isPoolCreationNonceUsed(address creator, uint256 nonce)
    external
    view
    returns (bool);
```

Nonce consumption occurs before external interactions. Any later revert restores the nonce with the rest of the atomic transaction.

Creator identity is immutable after successful registration.

## Atomic creation sequence

A successful general pool creation performs one atomic transition:

1. Enforce the liquidity pause and deadline.
2. Validate token addresses, tick spacing, normalized price, and requested fee rate.
3. Resolve the independent pool creation fee.
4. If the fee is zero, require the Diamond owner and zero `msg.value`.
5. If the fee is nonzero, require exact `msg.value` from every caller.
6. Construct the sorted PoolKey using native fee zero and the installed Statics hook.
7. Calculate PoolId.
8. Reject an existing basket or general pool using that PoolId.
9. Reject an existing hook registration or PoolManager initialization.
10. Resolve the direct, governed, or signed creator-authorization path.
11. Consume the unordered nonce when signed authorization is used.
12. Collect the creation fee to treasury.
13. Record the general pool and immutable creator.
14. Register the PoolKey with `StaticsSwapFeeHook`.
15. Store the initial PoolId-specific Statics fee rate.
16. Initialize the pool through PoolManager.
17. Emit the authoritative creation event.

Any failure reverts nonce consumption, creation-fee transfer, creator registration, fee-rate registration, hook registration, and PoolManager initialization together.

Hook registration must occur before PoolManager initialization.

Only the Diamond may register pools with the Statics hook.

## Market creation and liquidity provision

General market creation does not require an initial liquidity deposit.

A successful `createPool` transaction establishes:

```text
PoolId
initial price
tick spacing
creator
Statics fee rate
hook registration
protocol registration
```

It does not need to establish permanent liquidity.

The market may initially have zero liquidity.

A creator, market maker, community, or other LP may subsequently provide liquidity.

This intentionally separates:

```text
CREATE MARKET
      |
      v
REGISTER + INITIALIZE
      |
      v
PROVIDE USER LIQUIDITY
      |
      v
TRADING BEGINS
      |
      v
STATICS FEES ACCRUE
      |
      v
POL GROWS
```

A convenience interface may combine market creation and user liquidity provision, but the liquidity must remain ordinary user liquidity unless the caller explicitly chooses a permanent-liquidity contribution path.

Basket canonical launch may retain its existing mandatory initial permanent liquidity.

## Native v4 fee policy

Every Statics protocol pool uses:

```text
PoolKey.fee = 0
```

The hook continues to reject any nonzero native LP fee.

This rule applies to both basket canonical pools and general pools.

There is therefore no second native fee stream competing with Statics accounting.

All compensated swap economics flow through `StaticsSwapFeeHook`.

This simplifies user fee presentation, routing calculations, LP economics, creator accounting, permanent-liquidity accounting, treasury accounting, and protocol solvency invariants.

## External liquidity

Statics does not need to prohibit direct Uniswap v4 liquidity provision at the hook level.

An external user may technically create a PositionManager position against an active Statics PoolKey.

Because native v4 LP fee equals zero:

```text
direct external LP
       |
       v
provides market depth
       |
       v
receives zero native v4 swap fees
       |
       v
receives zero Statics LP rewards unless staked through Statics
```

Such liquidity can benefit execution but receives no swap-fee compensation from the protocol.

The protocol therefore has no economic reason to add complicated hook-level authorization handshakes solely to reject it.

Statics LP compensation remains opt-in.

## Statics LP positions

To receive the Statics liquidity-provider allocation, an LP PositionManager NFT must enter the Statics reward system.

Rewarded liquidity must remain **full range**.

For any PoolKey:

```solidity
tickLower = TickMath.minUsableTick(key.tickSpacing);
tickUpper = TickMath.maxUsableTick(key.tickSpacing);
```

This restriction is preserved.

Allowing arbitrary concentrated positions to receive Statics LP rewards would require the protocol to determine which positions were active at every swap and tick crossing.

That would duplicate significant portions of Uniswap's concentrated-liquidity fee-accounting problem and materially increase audit surface.

Statics instead uses a simpler invariant:

> Every unit of liquidity eligible for the Statics LP fee allocation is continuously active across the pool's full usable range.

Tick spacing may differ between pools, but the eligible reward range is always the full usable range for that PoolKey.

## Statics LP liquidity path

Reward-eligible user liquidity is managed through the Statics Diamond and `StaticsLiquidityManager`.

The liquidity manager must continue to:

- receive a typed PoolKey request;
- verify the exact PoolKey against the registered protocol pool;
- execute PositionManager operations;
- use transaction-scoped approvals;
- enforce token debit limits;
- refund unused assets; and
- accept calls only from the Diamond.

A user may either mint a compatible full-range PositionManager NFT and then stake it through Statics, or use a convenience Statics flow that mints and stakes the full-range position atomically.

Once staked, increases to rewarded liquidity continue through the Diamond and liquidity manager.

Unstaking returns the PositionManager NFT to the authorized user.

## Fee allocation profiles

The creator controls the market fee rate, not the distribution of the fee.

Statics maintains independently configurable protocol-controlled profiles for basket and general pools. The initial values and 9,500-bps configurable-share invariant are defined in [Fee configuration separation](#fee-configuration-separation).

Conceptually:

```text
BASKET POOL
Statics fee
  -> POL
  -> Statics LPs
  -> Basket stakers
  -> global Statics stakers
  -> creator, fixed 5%
  -> treasury
```

```text
GENERAL POOL
Statics fee
  -> POL
  -> Statics LPs
  -> global Statics stakers
  -> creator, fixed 5%
  -> treasury
```

General pools have no basket-staker allocation. Their profile makes this explicit rather than depending on the absence of a basket as an accidental runtime fallback.

Governance may reconfigure POL, LP, basket-staker where applicable, global Statics-staker, and treasury shares. Governance cannot change the fixed 500-bps creator share. Every accepted profile plus the creator constant must sum exactly to 10,000 bps.

## Creator revenue

Every protocol pool resolves to one immutable creator.

For basket canonical pools, the creator is the basket creator. For general pools, the creator is recorded during `createPool`.

Creator revenue is calculated only from the Statics bilateral fee. There is no native v4 LP fee to include.

Creator revenue is pull-based. Swap execution never makes an arbitrary external call to the creator.

A dedicated namespaced revenue store records both point credits and aggregate liabilities:

```solidity
mapping(address creator => mapping(address asset => uint256 amount))
    creatorCredit;
mapping(address asset => uint256 amount)
    totalCreatorCredit;
```

The aggregate is required for custody and invariant reconciliation because creator mappings cannot be enumerated.

The complete non-POL distribution is pulled from the hook and reserved once under `LibCustody.feeAccount()`. Creator credit is one liability within that reservation, separate from LP, basket-staker, Statics-staker, and treasury liabilities.

The claim surface is conceptually:

```solidity
function claimCreatorRevenue(
    address asset,
    address receiver,
    uint256 minReceived
) external returns (uint256 amount, uint256 received);
```

A claim:

1. uses `msg.sender` as the credited creator;
2. rejects a zero receiver;
3. clears the creator credit and decreases the aggregate liability before transfer;
4. pays through `LibCustody.pushReserved` from the fee account;
5. enforces `minReceived`; and
6. emits nominal and measured received amounts.

The claim path is non-reentrant. Any transfer or minimum-output failure reverts the liability changes and leaves the credit intact.

Creator credits do not expire. Governance cannot confiscate an accrued creator credit. Decommissioning does not alter accrued creator credits. Selecting a receiver does not change creator identity.

## Fee-routing interface

Fee routing should use a struct-based surface to remain compatible with the repository's non-IR codegen conventions and to avoid growing stack pressure as creator accounting is added.

Conceptually:

```solidity
struct ProtocolFeeDistribution {
    uint256 liquidityProvider;
    uint256 basketStaker;
    uint256 staticsStaker;
    uint256 creator;
    uint256 treasury;
}

function routeProtocolSwapFees(
    PoolId poolId,
    address asset,
    ProtocolFeeDistribution calldata distribution
) external;
```

The route:

1. requires the installed Statics hook as caller;
2. resolves the PoolId through the normalized protocol-pool registry;
3. verifies the reward asset belongs to the registered PoolKey;
4. accrues LP rewards by PoolId;
5. accrues basket-staker rewards only for basket canonical pools;
6. accrues global Statics-staker rewards by exact token address;
7. credits creator revenue to the resolved immutable creator;
8. accrues treasury revenue; and
9. preserves exact custody reservation accounting.

No creator or user callback occurs during the swap.

## Permanent liquidity

Permanent-liquidity inventory remains PoolId-local.

For each pool:

```text
polPending[poolId][currency0]
polPending[poolId][currency1]
```

tracks its own fee-funded inventory.

Two pools sharing the same currencies cannot spend each other's POL inventory.

For example:

```text
NVDA/USDC spacing 10
    -> independent NVDA/USDC POL

NVDA/USDC spacing 60
    -> independent NVDA/USDC POL

NVDA/WETH spacing 60
    -> independent NVDA/WETH POL
```

When both sides of a pool contain matched pending inventory, the hook may compound it into full-range permanent liquidity during normal swap execution.

A general pool can begin with zero permanent liquidity.

Its first full-range protocol-owned position may be created entirely from accrued Statics fees.

The permanent position uses the full usable range derived from that pool's selected tick spacing.

Creator rights do not include any ownership claim over POL.

## LP fee routing

The hook computes the complete Statics fee for the realized swap leg and allocates it according to the relevant pool profile.

For a general pool:

```text
total Statics fee
    =
POL
+ eligible Statics LP rewards
+ global Statics-staker rewards
+ creator revenue
+ treasury
```

For a basket pool:

```text
total Statics fee
    =
POL
+ eligible Statics LP rewards
+ basket-staker rewards
+ global Statics-staker rewards
+ creator revenue
+ treasury
```

If no eligible Statics LP liquidity exists, the protocol-defined LP fallback applies.

Direct external Uniswap liquidity does not become eligible merely because it is active in PoolManager.

Statics LP eligibility is determined only by the Statics reward system.

## Global Statics-staker rewards

Global Statics-staker rewards remain keyed by exact ERC-20 reward-asset address, not PoolId.

This differs intentionally from LP rewards and POL.

Suppose all of these markets exist:

```text
NVDA / USDC / spacing 10
NVDA / USDC / spacing 60
NVDA / WETH / spacing 60
TechnologyBasket / NVDA
```

Each may generate NVDA-denominated Statics-staker revenue.

Those amounts converge into the global reward book for the exact NVDA token address.

Eligible Statics positions opted into NVDA participate in the combined flow.

By contrast:

```text
LP rewards -> PoolId-local
POL        -> PoolId-local
creator    -> resolved pool creator
```

This distinction allows the broader DEX to feed a shared global staking layer without merging individual market accounting.

## Duplicate economic markets

Statics deliberately permits multiple pools for the same economic pair when tick spacing differs.

There is no general-pool canonicality rule based solely on which market was created first.

Different markets may compete on:

- tick spacing;
- Statics fee rate;
- available liquidity;
- permanent liquidity;
- market-maker participation;
- routing quality; and
- organic order flow.

Because fee rate is not part of PoolKey, the following are not distinct:

```text
NVDA/USDC
tickSpacing=60
Statics fee=20 bps
```

and an attempted new pool:

```text
NVDA/USDC
tickSpacing=60
Statics fee=30 bps
```

They resolve to the same PoolId.

A second pool requires a different supported tick spacing.

The existing pool's fee rate may instead be changed through whatever governed fee-control policy remains active.

Price discrepancies between independent pools are ordinary arbitrage opportunities.

Statics does not guarantee that adding more pools produces positive economic value. Routers should choose or split across venues according to execution quality.

## Pool creator authority

Creator status is economic, not administrative.

A creator may:

- establish a permitted general market;
- choose its initial valid tick spacing;
- choose its initial Statics fee rate within protocol bounds;
- provide liquidity;
- stake eligible full-range liquidity;
- receive the 5% creator allocation; and
- claim accrued creator revenue.

Creator status does not allow the creator to:

- change fee-allocation shares;
- bypass protocol fee bounds;
- change PoolKey after initialization;
- change tick spacing after initialization;
- replace the Statics hook;
- release permanent liquidity;
- withdraw protocol-owned assets;
- pause Statics;
- decommission the pool;
- alter global reward eligibility;
- confer basket status;
- confer collateral or lending status; or
- access basket or Dollar backing.

During the governed phase, governance may retain protocol-level fee-rate and decommission authority.

## General-pool decommissioning

The Diamond owner retains an explicit terminal general-pool decommission operation during the governed phase.

The creator cannot decommission the pool.

Decommissioning:

1. requires an active general pool;
2. marks the PoolId decommissioned in the hook;
3. prevents subsequent Statics-hook swaps;
4. prevents new Statics LP staking and increases;
5. stops new POL compounding;
6. releases protocol-owned permanent liquidity according to the supported treasury lifecycle;
7. preserves existing creator credits;
8. preserves previously earned LP rewards; and
9. never seizes external user LP principal.

Decommissioning is irreversible for that PoolKey.

Uniswap v4 initialization cannot be undone.

Creating a replacement market therefore requires a different supported PoolKey, which under this ADR generally means a different tick spacing.

Basket canonical pools retain their separate ExitOnly lifecycle and are never processed using general-pool decommission accounting.

## Views and indexing

The Diamond continues to expose bounded point lookups:

```solidity
function protocolPool(PoolId poolId)
    external
    view
    returns (ProtocolPoolView memory);

function isProtocolPool(PoolId poolId)
    external
    view

```

A general-pool view should expose at least:

```text
PoolId
PoolKey
creator
tick spacing
current Statics fee rate
decommissioned state
permanent liquidity
```

The authoritative creation event should include market configuration that is not encoded in PoolKey, particularly the Statics fee rate.

For example:

```solidity
event ProtocolPoolCreated(
    PoolId indexed poolId,
    address indexed creator,
    address indexed currency0,
    address currency1,
    int24 tickSpacing,
    uint16 inputFeeBps,
    uint16 outputFeeBps,
    uint160 sqrtPriceX96,
    int24 tick

```

The event does not need a native LP fee field because the Statics invariant requires it to equal zero.

Indexers reconstruct and rank markets from protocol-pool creation, basket-pool creation, fee-rate changes, creator credits and claims, POL events, LP staking and reward events, decommission events, and PoolManager state.

The first-party Statics frontend may maintain curated token lists and hide spam without changing contract-level permissionlessness.

## Routing

The existence of several PoolIds for the same pair requires an opinionated router.

Routing should consider at least:

```text
current liquidity
price impact
tick spacing
current Statics fee rate
pool state
multi-hop alternatives
```

A router may split a trade across multiple Statics pools when doing so improves execution.

Pool creation order does not imply routing priority.

Basket canonical pools and general pools participate in the same routing graph.

## Compiler and codegen constraints

The repository default profile compiles Statics-owned contracts without global IR.

Implementation of this ADR must preserve that architecture.

Global `via_ir` must not be reintroduced to accommodate permissionless pools, creator revenue, fee routing, or deployment changes.

Statics-owned code should instead use codegen-resilient patterns such as:

- request and result structs;
- scoped execution phases;
- narrow internal helpers;
- storage-backed intermediate state where appropriate; and
- facet decomposition where a coherent surface would otherwise exceed runtime or stack constraints.

Scoped IR exceptions remain limited to justified third-party dependencies configured by the repository and are not an acceptable substitute for refactoring Statics-owned code.

New or expanded facets must remain below the EIP-170 runtime bytecode limit with reasonable headroom under the default compiler profile.

Quote and routing interfaces introduced by this ADR should prefer struct returns and struct arguments where that reduces stack and ABI-encoder pressure.

## Compatibility and implementation approach

This architecture is deployed through a fresh protocol deployment. Existing governed-pool deployments and storage do not require migration or compatibility handling.

Implementation therefore uses the cleaner final architecture directly:

```text
ProtocolPoolKind.Governance
    -> ProtocolPoolKind.General

GovernancePool storage
    -> GeneralPool storage with immutable creator

quoteGovernancePool
    -> quotePool

createGovernancePool
    -> createPool

decommissionGovernancePool
    -> decommissionGeneralPool

fixed tick spacing
    -> creator-selected valid tick spacing

mandatory general-pool POL seed
    -> no mandatory seed

combined per-pool rate/allocation configuration
    -> PoolId-local fee rate + configurable global class profiles

no creator routing
    -> immutable creator + fixed 5% pull-credit accounting

governance-only generic creation
    -> independent creation-fee-gated permissionless creation
```

The removed governance-pool selectors are not retained as aliases. Upgrade migration scripts and legacy storage compatibility branches are out of scope.

The normalized general-pool registry should use a fresh namespaced storage version, such as `statics.storage.protocol.pools.v2`, so its layout cannot be confused with the replaced governance-pool registry.

The existing Statics hook's zero-native-fee rule remains. Existing full-range reward eligibility remains.

The generic liquidity manager remains conceptually appropriate because it already resolves exact registered PoolKeys rather than requiring basket-specific identity.

Existing governed-pool tests should be refactored where they already cover generic PoolId resolution, decommissioning, LP lifecycle, token movement, and fee routing. They are not compatibility tests for an old ABI or deployment.

The merged basket surface is split across `BasketCreationFacet`, `BasketMintFacet`, `BasketRedemptionFacet`, and `BasketViewFacet`; implementation and regression work must use those current facet boundaries rather than the removed monolithic `BasketFacet`.

The separate Doppler Genesis subsystem is unaffected.

## Security and trust boundaries

- Every Statics protocol PoolKey has zero native Uniswap v4 LP fee.
- Every Statics protocol pool uses the installed Statics hook.
- Only the Diamond may register PoolKeys with the hook.
- Exact PoolKey duplicates cannot create a second Statics market or creator.
- Tick spacing is creator-selectable only within valid PoolManager bounds.
- Initial Statics fee rates are creator-selectable only within canonical protocol fee bounds.
- Creator authorization binds PoolId, normalized price, fee rate, creator, nonce, and deadline under the Diamond's EIP-712 domain.
- Relayed creator authorization supports EOAs and ERC-1271 creators.
- Unordered creator nonces can be invalidated without serializing independent launches.
- Pool creators cannot change revenue allocation.
- Governance may configure basket and general allocation profiles only when their non-creator shares total 9,500 bps.
- The creator share is fixed at 500 bps and cannot be changed through profile administration.
- Pool creators have economic rights but no protocol-administrative authority.
- The pool creation fee is independent from basket and PositionNFT creation fees.
- Zero pool creation fee disables permissionless creation.
- Nonzero creation fee requires exact payment from every caller.
- Pool registration is not token endorsement.
- A malicious pool cannot gain access to basket or Dollar backing.
- Pool prices are not protocol solvency inputs under this ADR.
- Direct external LP positions cannot earn Statics LP rewards without entering the Statics staking system.
- Full-range-only Statics LP eligibility avoids tick-aware reward accounting.
- One PoolId cannot consume another PoolId's POL inventory.
- Creator credits, LP liabilities, Statics-staker liabilities, basket liabilities, and treasury liabilities remain separately solvent.
- Decommissioning never seizes user LP principal.
- Disabling creation never disables existing-market exits or claims.
- Statics-owned implementation code must continue compiling under the default non-IR profile.

### Public onchain visibility

Permissionless general pools are fully public onchain constructs and carry no
privacy guarantees:

- The immutable creator address bound into each pool's EIP-712 authorization is
  recorded permanently and is publicly readable through `protocolPoolCreator`
  and the `ProtocolPoolCreated` event.
- Creation transactions, the relayer/`msg.sender` that submitted them, consumed
  and invalidated nonces, every swap, every LP action, and every
  `claimCreatorRevenue` call are permanently public and linkable to the same
  creator and participant addresses.
- Because a general pool cannot be reassigned to a new creator, creator identity
  and its complete revenue history are permanently correlatable.

Integrators and creators who want to separate a market's onchain identity from
other activity should use a fresh creator address (or a dedicated smart-contract
wallet) per pool. Statics provides no mixing, shielding, or unlinkability layer,
and none of these public artifacts can be redacted after the fact.

## Required invariants

1. A PoolId belongs to at most one Statics protocol-pool class.
2. Every protocol-pool record hashes to the exact PoolKey registered with the Statics hook.
3. Every Statics protocol PoolKey uses `fee == 0`.
4. Every Statics protocol PoolKey uses the installed Statics hook.
5. Every general pool uses two distinct non-native ERC-20 currencies.
6. Every general pool uses tick spacing in the inclusive range 1 through 32,767.
7. Every normalized initial price is at least `TickMath.MIN_SQRT_PRICE` and less than `TickMath.MAX_SQRT_PRICE`.
8. Exact duplicate PoolKeys cannot create a second creator identity.
9. Distinct tick spacings for the same pair may create distinct PoolIds.
10. A different Statics fee rate alone cannot create a distinct PoolId.
11. A different initial price alone cannot create a distinct PoolId.
12. Every accepted fee rate satisfies `inputFeeBps + outputFeeBps <= 200`.
13. When `poolCreationFeeAmount == 0`, only the Diamond owner may create a general pool and `msg.value` must be zero.
14. When `poolCreationFeeAmount > 0`, every caller supplies exactly the configured amount.
15. The pool creation fee is independent from basket and PositionNFT creation fees.
16. Pool creation-fee changes affect only future creation.
17. Public creator attribution cannot be stolen by copying or front-running an authorized creation transaction.
18. Signed creator authorization binds PoolId, normalized price, fee rate, creator, nonce, and deadline under the current chain and Diamond.
19. A consumed or invalidated creator nonce cannot authorize creation.
20. Independent creator authorizations do not require sequential nonce execution.
21. Every active protocol pool resolves to exactly one immutable creator.
22. Creator revenue equals exactly 500 bps of collected Statics bilateral fees,
    computed by floor division per fee leg; sub-wei rounding dust from that
    division accrues to treasury rather than the creator, so at dust-scale
    `charged` the credited creator share may floor to zero while treasury absorbs
    the remainder. No other party is ever underpaid and totals always conserve.
23. Every configurable basket or general allocation profile sums to exactly 9,500 bps.
24. Every complete allocation including the fixed creator share sums to exactly 10,000 bps.
25. Allocation-profile changes affect subsequent accrual only and never rewrite accrued liabilities.
26. General pools never accrue basket-staker rewards.
27. Unavailable LP and basket-staker allocations follow the defined PoolId-local POL fallback.
28. Unavailable global Statics-staker allocations follow the defined treasury fallback.
29. Creator allocations never fall back and always credit the immutable creator.
30. Direct unstaked liquidity receives no Statics LP-reward allocation.
31. Every Statics-reward-eligible LP position is full range for its PoolKey.
32. Statics LP rewards remain PoolId-local.
33. Global Statics-staker rewards aggregate by exact reward-token address.
34. Permanent-liquidity inventory remains isolated by PoolId and currency.
35. A general pool may begin with zero permanent liquidity.
36. Fee activity may create a general pool's first permanent-liquidity position.
37. Pool registration alone never grants basket, collateral, lending, oracle, or Dollar privileges.
38. Creator revenue is credited rather than externally transferred during swap execution.
39. Aggregate creator credit by asset equals the sum of outstanding creator liabilities for that asset.
40. Creator claims debit only the creator's credit and the shared fee-account reservation.
41. General-pool decommissioning cannot debit basket backing or user LP principal.
42. Existing creator credits and earned reward liabilities survive decommissioning.
43. Disabling permissionless creation does not affect swaps, LP exits, creator claims, or reward claims on existing pools.
44. Existing basket canonical behavior remains unchanged except for the fixed creator allocation and normalized fee routing explicitly adopted here.
45. Statics-owned contracts introduced or modified for this ADR compile under the repository's default non-IR profile.
46. New or expanded facets remain below the EIP-170 runtime code-size limit under the default build profile.

## Consequences

- Statics becomes a permissionless Uniswap v4-native market layer rather than limiting its liquidity system to basket-associated or governance-selected pools.
- Basket markets become specialized first-class markets inside a broader Statics DEX.
- Statics retains one coherent swap-fee system instead of layering custom protocol fees on top of native Uniswap LP fees.
- All compensated LP economics remain visible and controllable through the Statics fee flywheel.
- Zero native LP fees create a strong economic incentive for LPs to use the Statics PositionNFT system without requiring complex hook-level restrictions on direct liquidity.
- Full-range-only rewarded liquidity keeps LP reward accounting simple despite allowing variable pool tick spacing.
- Creators can choose both market granularity and initial fee level without controlling protocol revenue distribution.
- A creator's 5% perpetual revenue share creates an incentive to establish, seed, promote, and route activity toward useful markets.
- A nontrivial creation fee discourages spam while also acting as the simple governance switch between controlled and permissionless creation.
- Multiple pools for the same pair may compete through different tick spacings, fee rates, liquidity profiles, POL accumulation, and routing quality.
- The protocol does not manufacture artificial duplicate PoolIds merely to support another fee tier.
- LP rewards and POL remain isolated by PoolId while global Statics-staker rewards aggregate across the larger market graph.
- General pools can bootstrap from user liquidity and organically grow protocol-owned liquidity from swap activity.
- Permissionless markets increase frontend and indexer responsibility for spam filtering, token verification, market ranking, and routing.
- Governance retains controlled-phase fee and lifecycle powers without granting those powers to individual pool creators.
- Those governance powers can later be constrained through the protocol's progressive-immutability process without redesigning the permissionless market primitive.
- The implementation must fit within the repository's no-global-IR and EIP-170 constraints, so feature work may require facet decomposition and struct-based interfaces rather than monolithic functions.
